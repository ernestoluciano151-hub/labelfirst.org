How we review

Written down so you can hold us to it, and so you can tell quickly whether a page elsewhere was produced the same way.

LabelFirstUpdated October 2026

What we check, in order

  1. The label. Ingredients as named by the manufacturer, doses where disclosed, serving size, and whether a proprietary blend hides the split.
  2. The claims. Each marketing claim separated into what is a permitted structure/function statement, what is verifiable, and what rests on nothing we can find.
  3. The evidence. Whether research exists on the ingredient, on the specific strain or extract, or on the finished product. These are three different things and we say which one applies.
  4. The terms. Price tiers, shipping, who processes refunds, and how long the guarantee runs. For products without published evidence, the guarantee is usually the only concrete thing on offer.
  5. What we could not verify. Every review carries this section. It is not a disclaimer, it is part of the finding.

We tell you when we have not used something

We have not personally used most of the products we cover, and we say so in the review itself rather than implying otherwise. A research-based assessment of a label, a set of claims and a refund policy is a useful thing. A fabricated account of a personal transformation is not, and it is the single most common form this category takes.

Where we have used a product, the review says that too, and describes the conditions.

What we will not publish

  • Invented testimonials, invented reviewers, or stock photographs presented as customers
  • Star ratings or review counts we did not collect
  • Claims that a supplement treats, cures or prevents a disease
  • Citations without a journal, a year and a participant count
  • Pages designed to be mistaken for a manufacturer's own site
  • Countdown timers, fake stock warnings or discounts that are not real

Any one of these would make a page convert better in the short term. All of them would make this site worth less than nothing.
