Affiliate disclosure

Plainly: we earn a commission when you buy through some of our links. Here is exactly how that works.

LabelFirstUpdated October 2026

The short version

Some links on this site are affiliate links. If you click one and buy, the seller pays us a percentage of the sale. You pay the same price either way — the commission comes out of the seller's margin, not added to your bill.

Which links

Affiliate links appear in the call-to-action box at the end of a review, and are marked underneath with the words "Affiliate link". They also carry rel="sponsored" in the page code, which is the standard machine-readable signal for a paid link.

Links to our own guides and to third-party sources are ordinary links and earn us nothing.

Who pays us

We participate in affiliate programs operated by retailers and networks, including ClickBank, which acts as retailer for several of the products we cover. We are not employed by, owned by, or paid a fee by any manufacturer whose products appear here.

No company sees our copy before publication. No company has the right to request changes. We have never removed a critical finding at a seller's request, and if that ever happens we will say so on this page.

The conflict, stated honestly

We earn more when you buy than when you do not. That is a real incentive and we are not going to pretend it has no effect on anyone who works this way.

What we do about it is structural rather than promised. Every review contains a "Skip it if" section naming the people who should not buy, and a "What we could not verify" section listing the gaps in the evidence. Both cost us money on every page. They are the test — if those sections ever go soft, the site is no longer worth reading, and you should treat their disappearance as the signal.

FTC

This disclosure is provided in line with the United States Federal Trade Commission's guidance on endorsements and material connections in advertising. It appears at the top of every page as well as here.
