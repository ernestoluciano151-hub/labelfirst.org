ProDentim Review: What's Actually In It, and Who It's For

A probiotic lozenge aimed at the mouth rather than the gut. We read the label, the claims, and the refund terms — and we are clear about what nobody can verify.

By Ernesto LucianoUpdated October 20266 minute readHow we review

Most oral-care products are built around removing things — plaque, bacteria, stains. ProDentim is sold on the opposite idea: that the mouth hosts a microbial population much like the gut does, and that adding certain strains to it may be worth doing.

That premise is not fringe. Research into the oral microbiome is an active field. Whether this particular product does anything useful for you is a separate question, and one this review cannot settle. What we can do is tell you precisely what is in the bottle, what the company says, what it costs to find out, and where the evidence runs out.

Worth considering if

  • You already brush and floss consistently and are curious about the microbiome angle
  • You want a low-commitment trial and value the 60-day refund window
  • You prefer a chewable to another pill

Skip it if

  • You have an active dental problem — pain, bleeding, loose teeth. That needs a dentist, not a supplement
  • You expect it to replace brushing, flossing, or professional cleaning
  • You are immunocompromised or pregnant, in which case probiotics warrant a conversation with your doctor first
  • You want published clinical trials on this specific formula. They do not appear to exist

What it actually is

ProDentim is a chewable tablet taken once daily, sold in 30-day bottles. It is a dietary supplement, not a medicine, and it is not a replacement for anything your dentist tells you to do.

The format matters more than it sounds. A capsule swallowed whole delivers its contents to the stomach. A lozenge dissolved in the mouth keeps them in contact with the oral cavity, which is the entire logic of the product. Whether that contact time is enough to change anything is exactly the part nobody has published data on.

What's in it

Taken from the manufacturer's own label, as published on the official page in October 2026.

IngredientWhat it is
Lactobacillus ParacaseiA probiotic strain studied in various contexts, including oral and sinus health
B. lactis BL-04®A trademarked strain of Bifidobacterium lactis, most studied for immune and gut outcomes
Lactobacillus ReuteriAmong the better-documented strains in oral-health literature
InulinA prebiotic fibre — food for the bacteria above, not a bacterium itself
Malic acidA fruit acid, commonly used for taste and sometimes associated with surface stain
Tricalcium phosphateA calcium compound used in dental products and as a tablet excipient
PeppermintFlavour

Total potency is stated as 3.5 billion CFUs per serving, across the three probiotic strains.

Worth knowing about that number. CFU counts are a measure of quantity, not of effect. A high count of strains that do nothing for your situation is still nothing. 3.5 billion is modest next to gut probiotics advertising 50 billion or more — but those are formulated for a different environment and the comparison tells you little either way. Treat the figure as a specification, not a selling point. We explain the convention in our guide to reading a supplement label.

What the company claims, and what we can check

Price and the refund window

Three package tiers are sold: a single 30-day bottle, a three-bottle package, and a six-bottle package. The multi-bottle tiers include two digital bonuses — Bad Breath Gone. One Day Detox and Hollywood White Teeth at Home. Shipping is free within the United States; international orders are charged $15.95.

The guarantee is the part worth reading closely. The company states a 60-day, 100% money-back guarantee, in its words: "If you are not impressed by the transformation of your gums and teeth… write to us and we'll refund every single cent."

Sixty days is long enough to actually try something daily and form a view. In practical terms it means the real cost of finding out is your time and the hassle of a return, rather than the price of the bottle — provided the refund process works as described. Purchases are processed by ClickBank, which handles refunds rather than the manufacturer.

What we could not verify

This is the section most reviews of this product leave out, so we'll be direct about it.

  • No published clinical trial on this formula. There is research on individual probiotic strains. There is nothing we could find testing this specific combination, at this dose, in this delivery format.
  • The named clinician. "Doctor formulated" appears without a verifiable name or stated relationship to the company.
  • Manufacturing specifics. We could not confirm the facility, third-party testing, or whether potency is guaranteed through the expiry date rather than at the time of manufacture — a meaningful distinction for live cultures.
  • Customer reviews. A large share of ProDentim "reviews" online are affiliate pages recycling the same copy, several on hijacked domains. We did not treat any of them as evidence, and neither should you.

On the last point. If you search this product you will find dozens of pages describing a dramatic personal transformation over 30, 60 or 90 days. Many are written by people who never opened a bottle. We have not used this product and are not going to pretend otherwise. What you have here is the label, the terms, and an honest account of where the evidence stops.

The bottom line

ProDentim is a transparently labelled probiotic lozenge with named strains, a modest CFU count, and an unusually long refund window. It is sold with marketing considerably more confident than the available evidence supports — which is true of most of this category.

If the oral-microbiome idea interests you and you treat it as an addition to brushing and flossing rather than a substitute, the 60-day guarantee makes trying it a low-stakes decision. If you are hoping it will fix a problem a dentist should be looking at, it will not, and the delay could cost you.
